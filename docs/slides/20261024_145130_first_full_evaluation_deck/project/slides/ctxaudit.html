The ceiling result invited a sceptical question: maybe the context judge is bad at finding facts in long legal text, or our oracle is too short. We tested both on a 57-question sample. The context judge passes the same kind of known-answer tests as the answer judge, at 98.5 percent balanced accuracy; it does not invent support from unrelated law; and when we hide the Q&A’s own source sentence in a 16k legal context it finds it 93 percent of the time. The 4k cut did matter: with a 16k oracle, support rises from 38 to about 50 percent, and a second judge agrees. So the ceiling was partly our oracle construction, but about 30 percent of vital nuggets remain unsupported by the cited law. The next slide classifies why.
