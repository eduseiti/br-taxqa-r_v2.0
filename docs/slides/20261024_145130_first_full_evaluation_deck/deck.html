2 · roadmap — The deck follows the order of a paper: question, data, method, evaluation design, results, robustness, then a scorecard of the hypotheses and the limitations. The robustness part is deliberately long because the dependent variable is produced by LLMs (nugget creator and judge), which is the first thing a reviewer will question.

Motivation

Statutes are trees, but RAG pipelines usually index flat pieces of them

Answers hinge on sub-article units

Official answers cite provisions like “art. 8º, II, b”. In our gold data, 2,230 cited provisions vs 1,958 cited articles.

Article chunks mix many rules

One article holds lists of cases, conditions, exceptions, tables for several years. Long chunks dilute ranking and eat the context budget.

Prior evidence stops at retrieval

Structural segmentation of Brazilian law was tested on retrieval metrics [Fernandes et al.] or qualitatively on 8 questions [Lima, 2024].

Gap

Does a finer, hierarchy-aware chunk give better answers, measured end to end against official gold answers?

[Fernandes et al.] Structural Segmentation for Brazilian Legal Document Retrieval · [Lima 2024] arXiv:2411.07739

3

3 · problem — Brazilian statutes have a fixed hierarchy: article, then caput and paragraphs, then incisos, alíneas and items, with groupings (parts, books, titles, chapters, sections) above the article. The official answers point at the lower levels. Fernandes et al. compared multi-layered segmentation for retrieval only and recommended article chunks with title and summary metadata; Lima proposed multi-layered chunks and showed a qualitative RAG case on the Constitution. Neither measured whether answers improve. That is the gap we address.

Motivation and hypotheses

4

4 · questions — H2 is the main hypothesis and the one the design is powered for. H1 is a precondition: if S2 does not retrieve better, an answer gain would be hard to explain. H3 to H6 are mechanism hypotheses. The timing matters for reviewers: H3 to H6 were written down in the plan note of 23 October before any answer was scored, so the null results for them are not post hoc. H6 is an interaction, which needs far more data than a main effect; we said in advance it might stay inconclusive.

Data and pipeline · v2.0 extends BR-TaxQA-R (HF: unicamp-dl/BR-TaxQA-R) with a parsed legal corpus

5

5 · data — BR-TaxQA-R v1 already had the questions and the references as text. Version 2.0 fetches every referenced act and parses it to LexML, the Brazilian XML standard for legislation, so each reference can be resolved down to the article or provision. The gold answers come from the tax authority, not from annotators, which makes them authoritative but also means they sometimes go beyond what the cited texts state; we return to that in the robustness part. The RIR/1999 was excluded after hand review of contexts: it is revoked, was cited once historically, and crowded out its replacement, the RIR/2018. The run with it is kept as an ablation.

Data and pipeline · chunk texts abbreviated (…); prefixes are the document title and ementa

6

6 · hierarchy — A concrete example of the unit choice. In S1 the whole article 35 is one chunk of about 720 tokens: all seven incisos and five paragraphs. In S2 the article chunk is kept and, in addition, every caput, paragraph, inciso, alínea and item becomes its own chunk, carrying the text of its ancestors (here the caput’s opening) but not its siblings. Every chunk is prefixed with the document title and its ementa, the official summary, which is the “core chunks plus metadata” setting Fernandes et al. recommend. The S2 chunk is about one fifth of the size and states exactly the rule the question needs.

7

7 · strategies — All five strategies are filters over the same chunk table, so the embedding model, the prefix and the index are identical; the only manipulated variable is which units are indexed. S2 keeps the article chunks, so it is a superset of S1. Paragraph chunks in S2 carry the article label but not the caput; that was a deliberate decision, and S2c and S4 test the alternative of adding the caput’s opening sentence. A full-caput variant, S5, is deferred. Non-statutory documents (rulings, opinions) are segmented by their own blocks and paragraphs in all strategies.

RAG pipeline

A plain dense pipeline, so that only the chunk unit varies

1 · Embed

jua-4B-mixed, a legal fine-tune of Qwen3-Embedding-4B; queries with a task instruction

2 · Retrieve

Dense top-1000 per strategy; no reranker, no hybrid

3 · Select

Rank order into a 4,000-token budget; drop or merge a chunk contained in a higher one

4 · Generate

Claims, each with a verbatim quote of ≤ 30 words; abstention allowed

5 · Score

LLM judge labels each gold nugget against the answer

Equal budget, not equal k

Strategies are compared at the same context size in tokens. Smaller chunks buy more distinct provisions, not more text.

A grounded prompt

Answer only from the numbered provisions; reference date tax year 2023; no value, law or year absent from them.

Pinned and logged

Model snapshots and providers pinned; tool use and web search refused; every call in a cost ledger.

Data and pipeline · embedding served on TEI, parity with the reference implementation: cosine ≥ 0.9992

8

8 · pipeline — We deliberately kept the pipeline simple: one dense retriever, no reranker, no query rewriting, so that differences come from the chunk unit. Context selection fills a token budget in rank order; if a chunk is contained in one already selected (an inciso inside its article), it is skipped or merged, so S2 does not waste budget on duplicates. Comparing at equal token budget rather than equal number of chunks is important: at equal k, S1 would simply get more text. The 4k budget is the middle of the retrieval range we measured (1k to 16k) and matches the oracle setting. The generator prompt asks for claims with verbatim quotes, which gives us grounding checks and citations to score. The embedding endpoint was checked against a local reference implementation before the corpus run.

9 · controls — Generators come in same-release size pairs from two vendors: Maritaca’s Brazilian-Portuguese models and OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 pair. The pairing exists to test H6, the size by strategy interaction. The three controls frame the strategies: closed book shows how much the model knows without retrieval (and the GPT models have a cutoff after the Q&A was published, so contamination is plausible); the oracle uses the cited articles as context and is the best a retriever could do with this corpus at 4k; the wrong-context control uses real legal text of an unrelated question and should score zero if answers come from the context. Evaluation design · nugget protocol after TREC RAG 2024 (vital / okay, support-based recall) · example paraphrased in English

10

10 · nuggets — We follow the nugget evaluation of TREC RAG 2024, adapted to the domain. A strong model splits each official answer into self-contained facts, each labelled vital or okay; the prompt forces exact values, a temporal anchor (the tax year) and forbids updating values to later law. A rule-based validator checks that every value in a nugget appears in the gold answer and that every answer line is covered. Two auditors from other model families then flag errors, and flagged nuggets go back for at most two repair rounds before the set is frozen. The judge comes from a third family to avoid self-preference. The example nuggets are paraphrased in English; the real ones are in Portuguese. The nugget set was frozen on 21 October, before the full RAG generation.

Evaluation design · tests: value ± step, a real value of another year, year shift, verdict flip, deleted span, removed condition, wrong answer, empty

11

11 · nuggetqa — Because the outcome variable is produced by LLMs, we validated both the nuggets and the judge before using them, against thresholds fixed in advance. Nugget agreement across three creator families is high. The model-based error estimate, nuggets flagged by two independent auditors, is 1.3 percent before repair. The auditors themselves were tested with planted errors. The judge was selected with known-answer tests: gold answers corrupted in controlled ways, including the most dangerous error in tax answers, a value from another year’s table. One gate was not met: three enumerated items in three questions remain uncovered after repair; they are listed, two are headings. Reviewers should note that synthetic corruptions are cleaner than real generator errors; the real-answer check comes in the robustness part.

Evaluation design · V_strict is the main metric of TREC RAG 2024 nugget evaluation

12

12 · metrics — V_strict is the headline: only full support counts, only vital nuggets count. The other variants matter for robustness: A and A_strict ignore the vital/okay label, so a conclusion that holds under them does not depend on that label, which is the least reliable part of the nuggets. Contradiction rate captures wrong answers, which recall alone hides. All comparisons are paired by question. We report both a bootstrap interval and a permutation p-value, and a cluster bootstrap over the 48 chapters of the Q&A because questions in one chapter cite the same acts. We did not adjust p-values; the S1 to S2 comparison is the single pre-specified main test, and the variant comparisons are reported as secondary.

Results · dense retrieval, containment de-duplication; paired differences from the retrieval evaluation

13

13 · retrieval — H1 holds clearly. At equal token budget, the S2 context contains about ten points more of the cited articles at every budget from 1k to 16k; at provision level the gain is four to six points. We had assumed strategies would converge at large budgets; they do not. S3 only differs from S2 at 16k, which is expected because its grouping chunks are large. The caput variants retrieve slightly worse at article level, but they were designed for the generator, so only answer scores can judge them. So the precondition for H2 is met: S2 gives the generator more of the right provisions.

Results · 714 paired questions · p from a paired permutation test

15

15 · s1s2 — This is the main result. The paired difference S2 minus S1 is positive for all four generators. For Luna and Sabiá-4 the interval excludes zero, with permutation p below 0.01, and the cluster bootstrap over chapters gives the same conclusion. For Terra the point estimate is positive but the interval touches zero under the primary nugget set; it becomes significant under both alternative nugget sets. Sabiazinho-4, the smallest model, shows essentially no gain. In relative terms, about ten percent for the two significant generators. So H2 is supported, with a small effect. The rest of the talk asks why it is small and whether it is real.

Results · 299 of 715 S3 contexts are identical to S2’s at 4k (same answers by construction)

16

16 · variants — The secondary strategies give null results. S3 adds grouping chunks, S2c and S4 add the caput’s first sentence to paragraph chunks. None of the differences is significant and the signs flip between generators. At 4k, S3 and S2 hold the gold equally often, so this is a fair test of whether explanatory grouping text helps the generator; it does not. One caveat for reviewers: 299 of the S3 contexts are identical to S2’s at 4k, which dilutes any S3 effect; the 16k point, where S3 also retrieves more, is planned. H6, the size interaction, shows no sign in the predicted direction, but an interaction needs far more data than we have, so we report it as not supported rather than as evidence of no effect.

17 · closedbook — The controls tell us what retrieval does here. Closed-book answers contradict at least one gold fact in 27 to 38 percent of questions; with retrieval it drops to 8 to 14 percent, for every generator. Recall improves a lot for the Maritaca models and moderately for Luna, but not for Terra, whose closed-book score is as high as with retrieval. Result 5 · why the gain is small

Two limits that segmentation does not touch

Method: the same judge labels the gold nuggets against the context instead of the answer. This splits answer recall into what the context holds and what the generator uses.

Limit 1 · what the context holds

0.48

of vital nuggets fully supported by the oracle context (S1 0.40, S2 0.41). S2 adds only +0.014 of content.

Limit 2 · what the generator uses

48–65 %

of the context-supported vital nuggets appear in the answer. Higher on S2 than S1 for three of four generators.

Check · leakage

2–6 %

of answer support is for nuggets the context does not support: RAG answers come from the context.

Answer follows context. Answer V_strict is 0.07–0.13 when the context supports no vital nugget, 0.26–0.34 when it supports some, 0.54–0.72 when it supports all (Spearman 0.53–0.67 per question).

Results · context support with prompt assign_ctx_v1; oracle = cited articles cut to 4k tokens (80 % of gold articles fit)

18

18 · ceiling — To understand the small effect we scored the contexts themselves: the judge checks each gold nugget against the context. Two limits appear. First, even the oracle context, the cited articles, fully supports fewer than half the vital nuggets at 4k; S2’s retrieval gain translates into very little extra content. Second, generators state only about half to two thirds of what their context supports. Leakage is low, so the RAG answers really do come from the context. And answer quality tracks context quality closely. The first limit raised an obvious question from a reviewer’s point of view: is that 48 percent an artefact of our oracle, our corpus or the context judge? The robustness section tests exactly that.

19

19 · decomposition — This decomposition is the mechanism result. Of 552 questions, the set of nuggets the context supports changes from S1 to S2 in only 73. Where it improves, answers improve; where it worsens, they get worse, as expected. But most of the overall S2 gain comes from the 479 questions where the context supports the same nuggets under both strategies. So S2 does not mainly bring new facts; it presents them in a form the generator uses better: smaller, focused units, and more of the cited provisions. The citation analysis agrees: answers cite more of the gold articles under S2. This also explains why the effect is bounded: segmentation changes form, not the content ceiling.

24 · ctxaudit — The ceiling result invited a sceptical question: maybe the context judge is bad at finding facts in long legal text, or our oracle is too short. We tested both on a 57-question sample. The context judge passes the same kind of known-answer tests as the answer judge, at 98.5 percent balanced accuracy; it does not invent support from unrelated law; and when we hide the Q&A’s own source sentence in a 16k legal context it finds it 93 percent of the time. The 4k cut did matter: with a 16k oracle, support rises from 38 to about 50 percent, and a second judge agrees. So the ceiling was partly our oracle construction, but about 30 percent of vital nuggets remain unsupported by the cited law. The next slide classifies why.

25 · prcontent — For the 60 unsupported vital nuggets we searched the whole corpus, lexically and densely, outside the cited articles, and had a strong model classify the cause with all the evidence; every classification was then re-checked against that evidence and 17 were changed. About half of the vital facts are fully stated by the cited law and a fifth in part. A small share was our own oracle construction. Synthesis · H3–H6 recorded before answer scoring

26

26 · scorecard — Summary in one table. The precondition and the main hypothesis are supported: S2 retrieves better and answers better, robustly but modestly. None of the mechanism hypotheses we recorded in advance is supported: grouping chunks, the caput sentence, period and condition effects, and the size interaction all come out null at 4k. The mechanism we did find is different from the one we expected: S2 helps mainly by giving the generator smaller, focused units of the same facts. And the reason the gain is small is mostly in the data: the official answers state things that their cited provisions do not, which no segmentation can retrieve.

Synthesis · threats to validity

What the evidence does not yet cover

Construct · LLM-made measurement

No human calibration yet of nuggets or judge; synthetic corruptions are cleaner than real errors.

Internal · one retrieval setting

One embedding model, dense only, answers at 4k only; Terra on S1 / S2 only (trimmed grid).

External · one domain, one year

Income tax 2024, one authority’s Q&A, Portuguese. Corpus = the cited law, not all IRPF law.

Contamination

GPT-5.6 may have seen the public Q&A. It inflates closed book, not the paired S1 / S2 contrast.

Ceiling audit is exploratory

57 questions, wide CIs; one question holds 12 of 60 cases; legal calls need a tax-law expert.

Small effect, few significant

Two of four generators on the primary set; no multiplicity correction for secondary contrasts.

Synthesis · 6 truncated answers counted as empty (changes no mean by more than 0.003)

27

27 · limitations — The limitations a reviewer will raise, grouped by validity type. The biggest is that the whole measurement is model-made: we have strong automatic evidence, but no human calibration sample yet; the 72 disagreements between the two judges are the natural starting point. Second, a single dense retriever and a single answer budget; a different embedding model or a reranker could change the size of the S2 effect. Third, generalisation: one domain and one year. Fourth, contamination: it matters for the closed-book interpretation, not for the paired comparison between strategies, which uses the same model and questions. Fifth, the ceiling audit is exploratory and small. Finally, the effect is small and only two of four generators are significant on the primary set; we present it as a modest, robust effect, not a large one.
